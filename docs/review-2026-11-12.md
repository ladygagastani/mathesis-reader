# Review of the new reader against the old one

Made on 12 Nov 2026 on a 1440 px wide window, in Paper and Night. The new site ran on the dev server (`npm run dev`) at
commit `cf1efe5`. The old site was read from its source in `_old-reference/web`, its `DESIGN.md`, the screenshots in
`screenshots/old` and the live reader at mathesisstoicheion.com. I did not build the old app locally. Its 59,000 lines need
their own install, and the live site runs the same code.

## Step 1. The new site, page by page

### Library (`/`)

Works:
- The search answers as you type, ignores accents, and finds Greek and English names.
- "Best match" puts the author you meant first.
- The address keeps the search and the order, so Back returns to the same list.
- An author with one work opens it in one click.

Does not work:
- The page's head takes 520 px. At 1440 × 900 the first author starts at 735 px, so the list, which is the point of the
  page, is below the fold.
- 371 authors in one column with no way to jump to a letter. Reaching Xenophon takes about 60 screens of scrolling.
- An author with several works needs a click to open, then a scroll, then a click. Plato's 37 works are in English
  alphabetical order with no Greek titles, so the Apology is the fifth row and the Republic the thirty-second.
- A work's row says only its title and "with English". It does not say how long the work is, how hard its Greek is,
  what kind of writing it is or when it was written.
- Nothing says where you were reading. A returning reader has to find the book again.
- The type hierarchy is weak. The author's name is 19 px, the description and the work's title are 14 px, and the
  counts are 12 px spaced capitals in a light ink.
- With no match the page says so and offers no way out.

### Reader (`/read`)

Works:
- Greek in Gentium Book Plus at 26 px reads well, and every letter is in the right typeface.
- The book opens on its text, with a title page the first time only.
- "Show the English" under one passage is a good way to check yourself.
- Passage references in the margin and section letters (17b) in the line are clear.

Does not work:
- The text column sits left of centre with a 400 px empty band on the right. The look-up appears at the far right edge,
  about 700 px from the word clicked, so the eye crosses the whole screen for every look-up.
- The line height is 1.75 at 26 px, so a 900 px window shows 14 lines of Greek. Long passages mean a lot of scrolling.
- "Both" squeezes Greek and English into the left two thirds and leaves the margin empty. The English column starts 10 px
  higher than the Greek, so the two never line up.
- The English shown under a passage is set at 16 px across the full measure. It is harder to read than the Greek above
  it.
- The reader forgets your place. Reopening a book always starts at page 1.
- The keyboard does little. The arrow keys move between words, but nothing turns the page, goes to a reference or
  shows the keys.
- You cannot go to a reference such as 29a, and you cannot find a word in the whole book, because only one page is on
  screen and the browser's own Find sees only that page.
- You cannot choose another edition or translation.
- A note's passage gets a hairline across the top of the page, which reads as a stray rule.

### Look-up (in the reader)

Works:
- Layer one has the full dictionary heading, the meaning, the grammar, the word's role and how common it is. Previous,
  Next and Note are in reach.
- Layer two has LSJ, Wiktionary, the word's family, how sure the analysis is and the credits.
- It never covers the line, and Escape puts the focus back on the word.

Does not work:
- It floats as a box at the top right, far from the word (see above).
- The grammar tags are rounded pills. Every other shape on the site has square corners.
- Layer two repeats the meaning and the grammar from layer one, so the new part starts below the fold of the box.
- There are no links to Logeion or Perseus for a reader who wants another dictionary.
- Wiktionary is fetched live, which sends the word to Wikimedia. `INVENTORY.md` dropped Wiktionary for exactly that
  reason ("no word leaves the site"). The current build contradicts it.

### Notes (in the reader) and Saved (`/saved`)

Works:
- A note on a word, line or passage in four colours. Noted words are underlined, noted passages have a bar, and marks at
  the right edge jump to each note.
- The foot bar's Notes lists the book's notes. Saved lists every note by book, with Open, Edit and Remove.

Does not work:
- The note editor needs "Save the note". Closing it with × loses what you typed.
- The four colour swatches wrap onto two lines in a 300 px box.
- You cannot note a phrase, only a whole word, line or passage.
- Saved uses a 740 px column on a 1440 px screen and shows only the reference ("λόγος in 18"), not the Greek the note is on.

### Sync (`/sync`)

Works:
- Clear steps, one action per button, each with a line that says what it does. The code is shown once.
- The header's Sync turns into Synced.

Does not work:
- The page is a 450 px column at the left of a 1440 px screen. "I have a code", the thing a returning reader needs, is
  below the fold.

### Private texts (button in the masthead)

Works: the list opens in place and its texts open in the same reader.
Does not work: nothing found.

### Credits (`/credits`)

Works: every source with its licence and a link.
Does not work: one narrow column. The page could hang its section names in the left margin and halve its length.

### The rest

- An address that leads nowhere shows Next.js's bare "404 This page could not be found" in Times on white.
- Paper and Night can be changed only inside a book. The library, Saved, Sync and Credits have no switch.
- The only way back to the library from Saved, Sync or Credits is the logo.

## Step 2. The old site: what a reader would notice

The old app had 51 pages. Most belong to rooms the inventory dropped (forum, map, wiki, lyre, vase studio, census,
manuscripts, Academy). The list below is what a reader of the library and the reader would meet.

**Reader.** Title page the first time, then a one-line title whose author name links to the author's page. Greek alone,
Both or English, remembered. Show the English per passage. Greek size stepper (px), line spacing stepper (1.4 to 2.2),
Paper, Night or Follow device, all in Aa and in Settings. Previous and Next page at the foot, titled with their keys.
Go to: a page menu and a typed reference ("Go to 1.33"), with a message that names a valid reference when one is wrong.
Your place in every book remembered (`lib/position.ts`) and offered back as "Reading now" and "Continue where you left
off". A "where you left off" marker beside the scroll bar. Notes "not translated" and "the English for these lines
begins on the page before". "Tap a word to look it up" at the foot of the page. Back to top. A light along the header
while the next page loads, and a fade between pages.

**Keyboard.** ← and → turn the page. `g` opens Go to. `?` shows the keys. `/` and Ctrl+K open the site search from any
page. Ctrl+F finds in the whole book. In the text: Tab in, ← → by word, ↑ ↓ by line, Home and End, Enter to look up,
Escape to close.

**Study menu.** Find in this text, Colour by case, Metre, Transliteration, Try it first (hide the English until tapped),
Vocabulary for this page, your notes and marks, Compare with another edition, a second book beside this one, Float the
reader, Places on this page, How it was written, Listen, Talk about it, choice of Greek edition and of translation,
which signs show beside the scroll bar, Keys, Home.

**Selecting words.** A toolbar on the selection: Bookmark, Favourite, Note, Highlight in four colours, Echoes, Share,
Look up. Notes saved as you typed, with light formatting, Greek typing and tags.

**Look-up.** Margin window on a computer, sheet on a phone. Layer one: form, kind, meaning, role, grammar, Previous,
Next, More about it. Layer two: LSJ with "The whole entry", Go further (where else in the book, how the sentence is
built, the lesson for this form, Word Study, the place on the map), Wiktionary, More dictionaries (Logeion, Perseus),
how sure, credits.

**Library.** "Not sure where to begin?" with suggestions by difficulty and six reading paths, remembered open or shut.
Search with the matched letters marked, and an "Authors" row for names that match. With English or Greek only. Orders:
By kind, Authors A to Z, Titles A to Z, By period. Filters folded under one line: kind of writing (with counts), period,
dialect, vocabulary (share of the 516 core words). Reading now. Open every author. A letter rail beside the list that
marks the letter you are reading under. Each work's Greek title, kind, date, length, a vocabulary meter, English or
Greek only, Best known, Start here, Reading at, About. "Nothing matches. Try fewer letters, search in English, or clear
the filters."

**Elsewhere.** Six famous books on the home page. Work and author pages. Settings on every page. A 404 page with a
search and the ways on. Offline copies. Sign-in. The Treasury: saved words, notes, notebooks.

## Steps 4 and 5: what was built

The owner approved the table's verdicts and the design on 12 Nov 2026. On the two questions: Wiktionary stays, live, sending
only the word (the owner, after the build); saved words are kept (the owner, after the build): "Save word" in the look-up keeps a
word with its meaning and where it was met, the Saved page lists the words, and they sync with the notes. Every "Bring" above is built, except as noted.

| From the table | Now |
|---|---|
| Place memory, Reading now, where you left off | A book opens at the passage you stopped at, in the edition you chose, with "You stopped here" in the margin. The library lists the books you are reading. |
| Keys | ← → turn the page, or step from word to word with a word looked up; `g` Go to; `?` the keys; `/` and Ctrl+K search the library from any page; ↑ ↓ Home End in the text. |
| Go to a reference, Find in this book | One Go to menu: a passage by its reference (a wrong one says what a reference looks like), a word anywhere in the book with the passages listed, and the pages. |
| Line spacing, edition and translation | In Text. The choice of translation shows only where a work has more than one. |
| English begins on the page before | Said in the English column. |
| Back to top | The bar's title takes you to the top of the page. |
| Paper/Night on every page, Library from every page, 404 | The site bar on every page outside the reader; a page for an address that leads nowhere. |
| Notes on selected words, kept as you write | Built. A note on a run of words underlines each word. |
| Logeion and Perseus | Links in the look-up's second layer. |
| Library orders, filters, rail, marks, rows, Where to begin, empty search | All built. Where to begin is computed from the vocabulary data (works with English whose words are most familiar), not a hand-picked list. |
| Highlights, bookmarks | Covered as planned: a note on selected words marks them, and place memory does a bookmark's work. No separate highlight or bookmark kind. |

Found while building, and fixed:
- Wiktionary's etymologies drew Armenian and Devanagari letters, and its vowel-length marks, in Times New Roman. The
  look-up now keeps a cognate's transliteration and drops the length marks.
- Chrome starts no text selection on an element that can take the focus, so the words now take a tab stop only while
  the keyboard is on them.
- NOTICE did not list Wiktionary, which the look-up fetches.
